Honest caveat: the paper does not publish its annual cash-flow workbook. This page runs an illustrative reconstruction: annual toll revenue from the paper's published traffic, tariff, growth and capex inputs, with two calibration scalars fitted to the paper's base NPV and capex sensitivity. The S1/S2 section-length split, the 20-year growth window and the absence of tax are my assumptions. The reconstruction gives a loss-making majority of trials, like the paper, but its P(NPV ≥ 0) is about 9%, not 4.68%. The paper itself has internal inconsistencies (table cross-references, a Section 2 range that differs between tables). The paper reports no outcome after the decision.

Run your own simulation

Set the value drivers, then run 3,000 trials. Continuous inputs use the paper's triangular shape, sampled by inverse transform. Optional: switch initial traffic to a normal shape (Box-Muller) and add correlation between the three sections' traffic through a Cholesky-based Gaussian copula. The copula is my extension: the paper simulates all inputs as independent. The lever sliders (tariff uplift, government capex support) go beyond the paper's tests.

The lesson

A project that clears its hurdle by 41 basis points has no margin for a forecast that is merely realistic. In this reconstruction, tariff and first-section traffic explain about 58% of the variance in NPV. Part of the paper's gap between a positive base case and a negative simulated mean comes from its own input shapes: the growth distribution has a most likely value of 85% of plan, not 100%. The paper reports no post-decision outcome, so what follows is a negotiating checklist, not a result.
